The thinking underneath

Four decisions that shaped all three pages

Search beats scrolling

Someone with a sick dog searches. They don't browse. The search bar is the first thing on the page, pre-filled with location.

Urgency is a feature

An emergency is a different job from a checkup. It gets its own module and a persistent call-now affordance.

Trust is visible

Ratings alone don't reassure. Registered status, response time, species treated and open-now state do the real work.

Mobile is the default

Most owners search on a phone, often standing up, often worried. Every layout is designed narrow-first and thumb-reachable.

For the reviewer

Where to find each graded skill

Mapped directly to the evaluation table in the brief, so nothing is a scavenger hunt.

Weight Skill Where to look Score
30% UI/UX & visual design All three prototypes share one warm-clinical design system — tokens, type scale, spacing and states defined once in assets/base.css.
20% Product thinking & CTAs Discovery page: emergency module, listing CTA and correction flow. Course page: one primary CTA repeated at each decision point.
25% AI workflow & automation Content System page: the six-stage pipeline, tool choices with reasons, prompt pack, and the editable sample carousel.
15% Technical execution No build step, no dependencies, works from file://. Filters, validation and carousel are real JS, not screenshots.
10% Initiative & communication The How I built it page: trade-offs, what I'd do with a second day, and open questions for the founder.
Honest scope note. This is a 4–5 hour design-and-prototype brief, so the three pages are front-end only: no database, no accounts, no real clinic data. Every provider, testimonial and statistic is illustrative. What is real is the interaction and the reasoning — the filters actually filter, the form actually validates, and the workflow page explains exactly which tool does which job and why.
